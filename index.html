About William

Thirty years in practice. One expensive education.

In February 2004 the biggest job his practice had ever taken on stopped paying, with £125,000 outstanding.

The practice held on, smaller, through the 2008 financial crisis; by March 2013 it was gone. What followed was the rebuild: Munich, then client side in social housing, responsible for the delivery of 250 units across 7 projects in 18 months, on time and under budget.

Alongside it, £25,000 invested in the business education architecture school never offered. Out of that came the PRICE Framework, the pricing and profit system his clients now run their practices on. Not theory. Based on 30 years in practice.

The arithmetic

The number you think you earn

£30,800 a year taken out
→
£22 to 28 an hour billed

The median sole principal takes £30,800 a year out of the practice (RIBA Business Benchmarking 2026). Spread over 1,100 to 1,400 billable hours (the range William sees most sole practitioners land in), that is £22 to £28 for every hour billed. One in four sole principals takes £17,043 or less (RIBA Business Benchmarking 2026, Table 4‑6). The gap is everything nobody counts. The unpaid site visit. The third round of amendments. The fee proposal that took a weekend. A practice that has never calculated its true cost rate is quoting blind, and a blind quote is nearly always low.

A worked example, from a rear extension

A job can be paid in full and still lose money.

The client paid the invoice in full. The practice still paid £1,480 to do the job. The bank balance never shows it. It shows only when the hours are set against what an hour of the practice really costs.
