Structural, not personal

We're trained to design, not to price. Architecture school taught detailing, planning, light, structure. It did not teach the true cost of an hour, or what to say when a client asks "can you just?". Across the industry, 17 to 22 per cent of every project runs past the agreed scope. Only around a quarter of that is ever billed. That pattern is not a character flaw. It is a missing system, and it wasn't your fault.

The gap is everything nobody counts. The unpaid site visit. The third round of amendments. The fee proposal that took a weekend. A practice that has never calculated its true cost rate is quoting blind, and a blind quote is nearly always low.

Learned the expensive way

In 2008, William's largest client defaulted on a major payment. A decade of work, nearly gone overnight. The financial crisis followed, and by March 2012 the practice he had run for 14 years was gone. What came next was the rebuild: Munich, client side, responsible for the delivery of 250 social housing units across 7 projects in 18 months, on time and under budget. Alongside it, £25,000 invested in the business education architecture school never offered. Out of that came the PRICE Framework, the pricing and profit system his clients now run their practices on. Not theory. Based on 30 years in practice.
