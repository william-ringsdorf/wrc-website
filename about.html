About William Ringsdorf

Thirty years in practice. One expensive education.

Architect Strategist. Ran his own practice for 14 years, designed over 750 homes, and learned the business of architecture the way nobody should have to.

The £125,000 lesson

In 2008, William's largest client defaulted, £125,000 outstanding. The financial crisis arrived the same year. The practice he had spent 14 years building held on for a while; by March 2012 it was gone. What followed was the rebuild. Munich, working with BMW, then client side in social housing, responsible for the delivery of 250 units across 7 projects in 18 months, on time and under budget. In the evenings, an education of a different kind: £25,000 invested in courses, mentors and hard-won mistakes, learning everything architecture school never taught about money.

A peer, not a guru

The work is strategy for architecture practices: pricing, scope, cash flow, and the structures that decide whether talent turns into profit. It is built on having run a practice, lost one, and rebuilt from the inside of the industry. We're trained to design, not to price. That gap is closable, and closing it is the whole job.

Three convictions

  • 1Fees are not a negotiation; they are a calculation. The number comes from your true cost rate, not from nerve.
  • 2Structural, not personal. A 7 per cent industry margin is not a profession full of bad businesspeople; it is a profession that was never taught the structure.
  • 3AI handles the production. You hold the judgement. The fee should follow the judgement.

The framework the collapse paid for

Five steps. Together they close the gap between the average UK practice margin of 7 per cent and the 15 to 25 per cent that law and accountancy firms consider normal.

  • PPricing Foundations. Know exactly what to charge, from your true cost rate, not guesswork.
  • RResistance Protocol. Protect margins from scope creep without damaging the client relationship.
  • IInvoice Architecture. Get paid on time by structuring payment before clients sign.
  • CCash Flow Clarity. A 13-week rolling forecast that takes roughly 15 minutes a week.
  • EEarnings First. Flip the equation: income minus earnings equals expenses, not profit as whatever is left.
